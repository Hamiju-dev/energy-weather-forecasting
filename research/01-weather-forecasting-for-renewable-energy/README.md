# Weather Forecasting for Renewable Energy

**Requirements by Technology, Department and Horizon, with Evidence from Three Global Models over Germany**

Emil Mierza · 2 November 2026 · Paper 01 · [**Read the PDF**](weather-forecasting-for-renewable-energy.pdf) (18 pages)

## Abstract

Wind and solar output, grid operation and electricity prices all depend on weather forecasts, but a forecast means something different to a trader, a commissioning engineer and a lender. The paper maps what a good weather forecast must deliver for each renewable technology, for each department across the asset life cycle and for each horizon from seconds to decades; collects the physical and statistical foundations in one notation; proves four results that govern forecast design; reviews weather-driven price formation, weather models (including the new machine-learning models), simulations, free data and their licences; and tests the results on 92 days of forecasts from ECMWF IFS, ECMWF AIFS and NOAA GFS against 62 German stations (May to July 2025).

## Key results

| Result | Value |
|---|---|
| Day-ahead 10 m wind RMSE, raw: IFS / AIFS / GFS | 1.37 / 1.36 / 1.46 m/s (IFS vs AIFS not significant, p = 0.45) |
| Same, multi-model mean with 30-day running bias correction | 1.12 m/s (17 % below the best single model) |
| Days of positive skill against climatology (daytime wind) | IFS 5.3, AIFS 5.5, GFS 4.3, bias-corrected multi-model mean 8.5 |
| Proposition 1, power-curve amplification | median power/wind error elasticity at partial load 3.18 (theory: ≥ 3) |
| Proposition 2, geographic smoothing | 62-station average keeps 27 % of single-station error; formula predicts 27 % |
| Proposition 3, when averaging two models helps | k² + 2ρk < 3; holds for raw IFS and AIFS (ρ = 0.90, k = 1.02), fails at 4 of 21 lead times after bias correction, as predicted |
| Proposition 4, optimal bid | bidding the 25 % quantile under 3:1 shortfall costs cuts expected cost by 25 % vs the point forecast |

All numbers are written by the code into [`paper/generated/`](paper/generated/) and are reproduced by `make analysis`.

## Reproduce

```bash
pip install -r code/requirements.txt
```

```bash
make analysis paper
```

`make fetch` re-downloads all inputs from the public archives (about 12 GB read, a few MB kept, 20-30 minutes). A TeX Live installation with `latexmk`, `pgfplots` and `natbib` is needed for the PDF.
